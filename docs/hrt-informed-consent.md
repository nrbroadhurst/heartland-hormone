## 3. Compounded or commercial product

My provider has told me which product I am receiving for the hormones checked above:

- [ ] A compounded formulation prepared by a compounding pharmacy
- [ ] A commercial FDA-approved product

The next three paragraphs apply only if a compounded formulation is checked.

A compounded preparation is not an FDA-approved drug product. The FDA does not review that preparation for safety, effectiveness, or quality in the way it reviews an approved medicine.

A compounded preparation can differ from a commercial hormone product, and from one batch to another, in strength, inactive ingredients, and how it is supplied. My provider has told me the formulation and dose planned for me.

If I want a commercial product and one is appropriate for me, my provider can prescribe that instead.

## 4. Testosterone for women is off-label

This section applies if I am a woman and testosterone is checked above.

FDA-approved testosterone products in the United States are approved for use in men. Testosterone is not FDA-approved for women for menopause symptoms, low sexual desire, low energy, or female hormone replacement. Prescribing testosterone to me is off-label.

This is off-label whether the testosterone is compounded or a commercial product. A commercial testosterone product is FDA-approved for men, and this use in women is outside that approval. A compounded testosterone preparation is not an FDA-approved product.

Off-label prescribing can be lawful when it is based on clinical judgment. It does not mean the FDA has approved testosterone for this use in women, and it does not mean a particular result is expected.

My provider has told me why testosterone is being considered, the dose planned for me, and the risks below. I may decline testosterone and still discuss estrogen or progesterone if those are appropriate.

## 5. Purpose

Hormone therapy is being considered to treat symptoms or laboratory findings reviewed with my provider. My plan is based on my history, symptoms, examination when indicated, and laboratory results.

No specific result has been promised. Energy, mood, sleep, sexual function, periods, body composition, and laboratory values may not change.

## 6. Evaluation and monitoring

I understand hormone therapy is offered only after a clinical evaluation. My provider may repeat laboratory tests and visits while I am treated and may change or stop a hormone based on my response, side effects, or results.

I will tell my provider about all medicines and supplements I use. I will tell my provider if I am pregnant, trying to become pregnant, or breastfeeding. I will tell my provider about any history of cancer, blood clot, stroke, heart disease, liver disease, or a serious reaction to a hormone.

## 7. Risks

Risks that have been explained to me include:

- Breast tenderness, bloating, fluid retention, headache, mood change, or acne
- Bleeding or a change in periods
- Skin irritation from a cream, patch, or troche, or pain, bruising, or infection from an injection
- Changes in cholesterol, blood count, liver tests, or other laboratory results
- For testosterone in women: acne, oily skin, facial or body hair, scalp hair thinning, voice deepening, enlargement of the clitoris, and effects that may not fully reverse after the medicine is stopped
- Blood clot, stroke, heart attack, or other cardiovascular events, which can be associated with some hormone therapy
- A possible effect on the risk of some cancers. My provider has discussed the cancer risks that apply to the hormones prescribed for me
- Differences in a compounded preparation from batch to batch, including strength or inactive ingredients
- Unknown or uncommon effects, including effects of compounded hormone preparations and of off-label testosterone use in women

This list does not include every possible effect. My provider has discussed the risks that apply to the hormones checked above and to my own history.

## 8. Alternatives

My provider has discussed alternatives, including no hormone therapy, an FDA-approved hormone product when one is appropriate, a different hormone or dose, non-hormonal treatment, and lifestyle measures. I may choose an alternative or decline any hormone, including testosterone.

## 9. My responsibilities

I will use each hormone only as prescribed. I will not share it, change the dose on my own, or substitute a product from another source. I will get the medicine from the pharmacy my provider uses.

I will contact the clinic about side effects that concern me and seek emergency care for severe symptoms such as chest pain, trouble breathing, one-sided weakness, fainting, or a rapidly worsening reaction.

I will keep follow-up visits and complete laboratory tests my provider orders.

## 10. Right to refuse or stop

I may refuse this treatment or stop it later. Stopping may mean the symptoms being treated stay the same or return. I will tell the clinic if I stop so my record and any monitoring can be updated.

Signing this form does not require me to start or continue treatment.
